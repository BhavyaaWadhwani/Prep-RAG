# Interview Preparation Notes - Sample Content

This is sample content for demonstrating the app. Replace it with your own notes and real interview experiences.

## Tell me about yourself
Use a present-past-future structure. Start with who you are now: your degree, year and main focus area. Then mention two or three projects or achievements that are relevant to the role, with one concrete result each. Finish with why you are interested in this role. Keep it to about 90 seconds and do not recite your resume line by line.

## Explaining your projects
Interviewers pick one project and go deep. For every project, be ready to explain the problem, why you chose the tech stack, the hardest bug or design decision, what you would improve, and how you tested it. If you list a skill on your resume, expect at least one question on it.

## DSA patterns to revise first
Start with the patterns that cover the most problems: two pointers, sliding window, binary search on the answer, hash map frequency counting, stack-based problems, BFS and DFS on graphs and grids, and 1D dynamic programming. After these, move to tree traversals, heaps, backtracking and 2D dynamic programming.

## HR round
Common questions include strengths and weaknesses, a conflict in a team, a failure and what you learned, why this company, and where you see yourself in five years. Use the STAR method (situation, task, action, result) and prepare three stories that can be reused: a leadership story, a failure story and a teamwork story.

## Salary and offers
Do not bring up salary in technical rounds. If asked about expectations, say you are open to the company's standard campus package for the role.
